Questions to answer with line-level evidence:
1. Gap fill rewrites bytes between dirty pages so one `pwrite`/`msync` covers a range. Prove
   every byte it writes is byte-identical to what the file holds (published pages, free pages,
   the preallocated tail): what is the source of the bytes (the mapping? a private copy?), and
   can a reader's snapshot or a concurrent remap/trim change the source between read and write?
   Can a gap fill write past the published length, into the trimmed tail, or over a meta page?
   What does a crash in the middle of a gap fill leave (torn gap pages are unchanged pages, so
   nothing; confirm the dirty pages themselves are still written whole)?
2. `auto` width: it is chosen from measured sync costs per process. Confirm nothing on disk or
   in the committed state depends on the width (two nodes with different widths hold identical
   files up to the tail), that a refusal (`NOWAIT` read refused, the fallback to `off`) is
   counted and logged, and that the chooser cannot oscillate into a pathological width (what are
   its bounds and step rule, and is `MAX_GAP_FILL` enforced on the flag and on auto?).
3. Boot record: what `decide` concludes in each of its cases (no record, a record of this boot,
   of another boot, unreadable, a CRC failure, a directory that refuses to create it), and
   whether a wrong decision can ever skip the cache drop after a failed writeback in the same
   boot (the M1 case of the #1759 review): trace the record's write points (open, close, sync?)
   and whether a crash between a failed writeback and the record's update leaves a record that
   says "clean".
4. The weak-head check's read-ahead: it issues reads ahead of the check; confirm an I/O error on
   a read-ahead is not swallowed into a passed check, and that the check still verifies exactly
   the pages the weak head names since the last sync (the `named_after` pruning, 2^31 wrap).
5. Format 8 and the free walk: the run tag comes from the leaf's header at decode; confirm every
   path that constructs an `Ovf` for a run it writes passes the right tag (nested trees:
   `nested_tag`; the catalog and free tree never hold runs), and that the free walk's
   every-leaf read cannot free a page twice or skip the overflow counter check; a `Durable`
   no-change commit's sync cannot run while another writer or a close is syncing (who holds the
   writer?).
6. Unsafe and I/O: new `unsafe` blocks (`sys.rs`, `file.rs`) carry `SAFETY:` comments whose
   claims hold; every new syscall error path keeps its OS error and fails closed or warns as the
   docs say (`PORTING.md` table).
7. The test suites: are the crash model's new lengths and the `AfterWeakMetaCopy` point modelled
   right (a process crash keeps the weak head, a power loss falls back)? Any test that asserts
   HEAD behaviour that is wrong?
